Eduardo Fernandes Montesuma

AI Researcher @ Sigma Nova. PhD, Université Paris-Saclay.

Paris, France

I am an AI researcher specializing in optimal transport, domain adaptation, transfer learning, and foundation models. I completed my PhD in Mathematical Computer Science at Université Paris-Saclay in 2024, where I focused on multi-source domain adaptation through Wasserstein barycenters. Prior to that, I earned a Master’s degree in Electronic Engineering and Industrial Informatics from INSA de Rennes and a Bachelor’s in Computer Engineering from Universidade Federal do Ceará in Brazil. You can take a look on my recent publications at Google Scholar.

Since August 2025 I work as an AI researcher at Sigma Nova, where I work on pre-training, fine-tuning, and transfer learning of large-scale foundation models for neuroscience and time series forecasting. My research bridges theoretical advances in optimal transport with practical machine learning applications, particularly in scenarios where models need to adapt across different domains or data distributions.

  1. arXiv
    Towards Universal Wasserstein Barycenters through Flow Matching
    Eduardo Fernandes Montesuma
    2026
    Under review
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF

    Defining a weighted mean over probability measures under probability metrics is a central tool in probabilistic machine learning. Under the Wasserstein metric, these are called Wasserstein barycenters. While most approaches compute barycenters for a fixed weight vector, approximating the whole family of barycenters over the simplex, which we call the Wasserstein simplex, remains underexplored. We refer to this problem as Universal Barycenter Approximation, and propose BaryFM, a flow matching model transporting the marginal measures into any barycenter in the Wasserstein simplex. Once trained, the network can draw samples from measures in the Wasserstein simplex through an ordinary differential equation. We validate our method on 4 downstream tasks: domain adaptation, generalization, Bayesian posterior aggregation and algorithmic fairness. BaryFM achieves the best average rank among 15 competing methods across 10 domain adaptation benchmarks, matching or surpassing non-universal solvers.

  2. CVPR
    ReBaPL: Repulsive Bayesian Prompt Learning
    Yassir Bendou, Omar Ezzahir, Eduardo Fernandes Montesuma, and 3 more authors
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Code

    Conventional prompt learning methods are prone to overfitting and can struggle with out-of-distribution generalization. We frame prompt optimization as a Bayesian inference problem and propose ReBaPL, which integrates a cyclical step-size schedule with a stochastic gradient Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (SGHMC) algorithm and a repulsive force derived from probability metrics (Maximum Mean Discrepancy, Wasserstein) to diversify exploration of the prompt posterior and prevent premature collapse to a single mode.

  3. UAI
    Wasserstein Gradient Flows for Scalable and Regularized Barycenter Computation
    Eduardo Fernandes Montesuma, Yassir Bendou, and Mike Gartrell
    In Proceedings of the 42nd Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence (UAI), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Code

    We present a method for computing Wasserstein barycenters via gradient flows in probability measure space. The approach yields a scalable algorithm that (i) relies on mini-batch optimal transport, (ii) accepts modular regularization through task-aware functions, and (iii) admits supervised information in the cost function. Experiments on domain adaptation tasks across computer vision, neuroscience, and chemical engineering show that incorporating label information improves over existing barycenter approaches.

    Recent Advances in Optimal Transport for Machine Learning
    Eduardo Fernandes Montesuma, Fred Maurice Ngolè Mboula, and Antoine Souloumiac
    IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, 2024
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Code

    Optimal Transport (OT) is a probabilistic framework for comparing and manipulating probability distributions. We survey the contributions of OT to machine learning over 2012-2023 across supervised, unsupervised, transfer, and reinforcement learning, including computational OT, partial, unbalanced, Gromov, and Neural OT and their interplay with ML practice.

  8. CVPR
    Wasserstein Barycenter for Multi-Source Domain Adaptation
    Eduardo Fernandes Montesuma and Fred Maurice Ngolè Mboula
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2021
    Abs Bib PDF Code

    Multi-source domain adaptation is a key technique that allows a model to be trained on data coming from various probability distributions. We propose a method for constructing an intermediate domain between sources and target domain via the barycenter on Wasserstein spaces (WBT). After aggregating sources, they are transported to the target domain using standard OT for Domain Adaptation. We evaluate on object recognition, face recognition, music genre recognition, and music-speech discrimination.
